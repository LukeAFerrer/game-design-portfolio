Risk of Rain 2: The Card Game

Solo project

A full physical translation of a roguelike video game's escalating-difficulty loop — designed, built, and balanced independently across multiple rounds of solo playtesting.

Risk of Rain 2 is built around one core tension: items and power stack up fast, but so does the difficulty. I designed a card game that reproduces that exact feeling at a table, with four playable characters, an enemy system that gets harder the longer you take, and a run that ends in a two-phase fight against the final boss.

  • Four character decks — Commando, Huntress, Bandit, and Engineer — each with its own basic attack, action-point economy, and skill cards
  • An Enemy Credit Counter that rises every round, so encounter difficulty scales automatically with player count and how long the run goes
  • A gold economy earned from kills and chests, spent on items that stack toward a run-ending showdown with the boss, Mithrix

Balance patch notes — from solo playtesting

v1.1

Reduced gold earned from kills and raised chest prices. Gold was coming in too easily and didn't feel like a resource worth planning around.

v1.2

Rewrote the wording on five cards players kept misreading mid-turn, including Double Tap and Mithrix's phase-three attack.

v1.3

Raised the enemy-scaling rate and buffed every boss. Early runs were winnable without any real pressure, which made the back half feel flat.

v1.4

Nerfed the Huntress's Ballista after it proved to be the strongest card in the game by a wide margin, and clarified how item stacking actually resolves.

Masterpieces for Acquisition

Team project

An auction-and-economy card game inspired by a visit to the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston — designed with a small team and reshaped after two rounds of playtesting.

Players bid on real museum artifacts across four categories, then put those pieces to work generating income and popularity. Each round moves through an open auction, an income phase driven by dice and a popularity stat, and an action phase where players upgrade their museum or trigger card effects.

  • A five-round bidding phase where every artwork's starting price is set by its own printed income value
  • A popularity-driven dice economy — your popularity score sets how many dice you roll for income each round
  • Four layered endgame systems — Magnum Opus events, museum upgrades, hidden Banksy cards, and milestone scoring — that give players different paths to the same win condition

Playtest log

Playtest 1

Players enjoyed the game once they learned it, but asked for more direct ways to interfere with each other, and felt the card variety ran thin.

↓ changed
Rebuild

Added a wave of player-interaction cards — stealing, discarding, flipping an opponent's artwork face down — and finished the Magnum Opus event decks.

↓ result
Playtest 2

The new interaction cards were the most-used mechanic in the room, and the game got noticeably more competitive.

Threw It On The Ground

Solo project — in progress

A 2–4 player card game about a crew of movers clearing an apartment the fast way — out the window. Currently in active development, with a full physical prototype built and two rounds of playtesting done.

Players pick items from their hand and throw them out a ten-story window, racing to score points before the Fun Police show up and shut the job down. Every item has a weight, which sets how many floors it falls, and a point value that depends entirely on what — or who — it lands on.

  • An 84-card deck split across three roles: 51 Items (what gets thrown), 29 Pedestrians (what it lands on), and 4 asymmetric Movers — optional characters with their own once-per-set ability
  • A physical ten-floor apartment board, built as a four-page print-and-play assembly with its own cut-and-tape instructions
  • A round structure split into three sets, where a hidden "Sidewalk" pile of revealed Pedestrian cards decides what every thrown item scores against — until the Fun Police card turns up and ends the game

Dev timeline — still in progress

Playtest 1 — Aug 2026

Confirmed the core premise was fun, but surfaced rules typos, a clunky falling-phase writeup, and a couple of cards (Wet Blanket, Fan) that weren't fun for whoever they hit.

Build — Nov 2026

Added dedicated draw, discard, and pedestrian piles, resized the board and cards to fit standard sleeves, and designed all four asymmetric Movers from scratch.

Prototype — Nov 2026

Printed, cut, and sleeved a full physical copy, and fixed the Wet Blanket and Fan cards flagged as unfair in the first playtest.

Playtest 2

The Movers landed well and added real replayability, though one proved overpowered. Reworked the Mirror item to simply duplicate a card instead of copying its stats separately, and renamed "Oncoming Traffic" to the clearer "Sidewalk" pile.
